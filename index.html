The Route

Driest desert to the end of the earth

Some birthdays you mark with a dinner. Some you mark with a drive across a continent. Two acts, two of the world's most extraordinary landscapes, zero filler. The Atacama — otherworldly, 14,000 feet, geysers before sunrise, the clearest skies on Earth. Then Patagonia — the Carretera Austral, the most beautiful lakeside road in Chile, Cerro Torre, the Towers, the Strait of Magellan, Ushuaia. No overnight ferry. No single point of failure. Every drive day on roads worth driving.

Structure

Two acts. One finish line.

I
The Desert
January 8–11 · Days 1–4
Fly into Calama. A local guide picks you up before dawn. Three days in the driest non-polar desert on Earth — no rental car, no logistics to manage. The Atacama is better with someone who knows where to be before sunrise.
El Tatio Geysers — 14,000ft before dawn
Valle de la Luna at dusk
Salar de Atacama, flamingos
Private observatory, night sky
II
Patagonia
January 12–23 · Days 5–16
Fly to Balmaceda. Pick up the 4WD. Drive south through the Carretera Austral, east along the most beautiful lakeside road in Chile, down Ruta 40 to Fitz Roy, into Torres del Paine, across the Strait of Magellan. Drop the car in Punta Arenas. Cross to Ushuaia.

Ruta 265 — lakeside drive to Chile Chico
Cerro Torre — Laguna Torre hike

The Plan

Day by day

Every day earns its place. No filler, no slog, no wasted hours on highway pavement. Built against real distances and real drive times — then pressure-tested.

01
Friday Jan 7 → Saturday Jan 8 · Atlanta → Calama
Wheels up. The trip begins.
Fly out Friday night after work. ATL → MIA or BOG → SCL → CJC. 18–22 hours of travel. Arrive Calama Saturday afternoon, one hour transfer to San Pedro de Atacama. 7,900ft. Walk the dusty main street. Early dinner. The sky tonight is already different from anything at home.
Sleep San Pedro de Atacama
Elev 7,900 ft
02
Sunday Jan 9 · San Pedro de Atacama
Settle in. Let the altitude do its work.
Don't push it. 7,900ft hits harder than you expect after 20 hours of travel. Walk the dusty main street. Eat something slow. The desert light in the late afternoon is already unlike anything at home. Early sleep — tomorrow starts at 4am and the alarm is non-negotiable.
Sleep San Pedro
Note Early bed — 4am departure tomorrow
03
Signature
Monday Jan 10 · El Tatio Geysers · 14,000ft
Steam rising before the sun. Nobody says anything.
Your guide picks you up at 4am. El Tatio at 14,000ft — columns erupting in the pre-dawn freeze while the Andes ignite behind them. Breakfast at the geysers. Afternoon: Valle de la Luna, salt formations, dunes at sunset. This is the reason the Atacama is in the itinerary.
Depart 4:00am
Elev 14,000 ft
Sleep San Pedro
04
Signature
Tuesday Jan 11 · Salar de Atacama · Night Sky
Salt flats, flamingos, then the clearest sky on Earth.
Morning: Salar de Atacama and the flamingo lagoons — pink birds on white salt beneath the Andes. Afternoon: free time in San Pedro, your last slow afternoon for a while. After dark: guided tour at a private observatory above the village. The Atacama hosts more world-class telescopes than anywhere on Earth. This sky is the reason.
Highlight Private observatory
Sleep San Pedro — last night
05
The Pivot
Wednesday Jan 12 · Calama → Balmaceda → Coyhaique
Fly south. Pick up the 4WD. Act II begins.
Transfer to Calama airport. Fly CJC → SCL → BBA (~5 hours with connection). Pick up the expedition 4WD from LYS at Balmaceda airport — high-clearance diesel, cross-border authorized. 45 minutes to Coyhaique. Resupply: fuel, cash, food. Dinner at Mamma Gaucha — centrally located, cozy, Patagonian lamb worth the stop. Sleep in Coyhaique. Tomorrow: the Carretera Austral.
Flight CJC → SCL → BBA
Vehicle LYS 4WD — BBA airport
Dinner Mamma Gaucha, Coyhaique
Sleep Coyhaique
06
Signature
Thursday Jan 13 · Coyhaique → Cerro Castillo → Puerto Río Tranquilo
The Carretera Austral opens up. Then the bluest lake in Chile.
Drive south on Ruta 7 as the pavement gives way to ripio. The road demands respect from the first mile — narrow, gravel, no guardrails, and conditions that have rolled vehicles. Drive deliberately. Villa Cerro Castillo rises ahead — dramatic granite spires above the village, worth the stop and the photo. Then on to Puerto Río Tranquilo on Lago General Carrera — 134 miles and ~5 hours of gravel in total. The water is an impossible shade of blue-green, and tomorrow you get on it.
Stop Villa Cerro Castillo — mirador
Drive ~5 hrs gravel total
Road Carretera Austral — drive carefully
Sleep Puerto Río Tranquilo
07
Signature
Friday Jan 14 · Puerto Río Tranquilo
Into the caves. Swirling marble lit from below.
Boat into the Marble Caves at first light — book with Pedro's tour operation in Puerto Río Tranquilo; his son Johnny runs the boats and knows these caves better than anyone. Best light is 9:30am when turquoise water reflects up into the carved ceiling and everything glows blue-white, though afternoon light (around 4pm) is also extraordinary. No photograph does it justice. No rush today. Afternoon on the lake. Sleep nearby.
Tour Pedro's boats — ask for Johnny · 9:30am
Sleep Puerto Río Tranquilo
08
Signature
Saturday Jan 15 · Ruta 265 → Chile Chico → Los Antiguos
The lakeside drive. 130 miles of cliffside road above turquoise water.
Drive east on Ruta 265 along the southern shore of Lago General Carrera. Cliffside road, the lake hundreds of feet below, the Andes overhead. Nobody talks about this road as one of Chile's best. They should. Cross at Paso Río Jeinemeni into Los Antiguos, Argentina. You're on Ruta 40. Sleep in Los Antiguos. Critical: Ruta 265 averages 25–30 mph on gravel. The Jeinemeni border crossing closes at 8pm. Leave Puerto Río Tranquilo by 9am and be at the border by 5pm.
Depart 9:00am hard
Drive ~130 mi · Ruta 265
Border Chile → Argentina · cross by 5pm
Sleep Los Antiguos
09
Long Drive
Sunday Jan 16 · Los Antiguos → El Chaltén
Ruta 40. The steppe opens. Then Fitz Roy fills the windshield.
Leave by 6am. ~370 miles south on Ruta 40 — expect 9–10 hours, not 6. The stretch between Gobernador Gregores and Tres Lagos is 70km of brutal washboard ripio. Fill the jerry cans in Los Antiguos and top off at every open station — Bajo Caracoles frequently runs dry. Keep speed below 60 km/h on gravel and both hands on the wheel: Patagonian crosswinds hit 60–80 mph on the steppe and have rolled high-clearance pickups. Then in the final hour Fitz Roy rises on the horizon and grows until you're there. The drive earns the arrival.
Depart 6:00am hard
Drive ~370 mi · 9–10 hrs · Ruta 40
Fuel Fill jerry cans in Los Antiguos
Sleep El Chaltén
10
MLK Day
Monday Jan 17 · El Chaltén · Martin Luther King Day
Laguna Torre. Cerro Torre. The federal holiday lands here.
Wake without an alarm. Hike Laguna Torre — 11 miles roundtrip, ~5 hours. Cerro Torre is a near-vertical granite needle that wasn't summited until 1974 and is still considered one of the hardest climbs on Earth. The glacial lake at its base is still and cold and completely surrounded by nothing. Back by afternoon. Craft beer in town. Early sleep. Tomorrow you drive.
Hike Laguna Torre · 11 mi · ~5 hrs
Sleep El Chaltén
11
Long Drive
Tuesday Jan 18 · El Chaltén → Puerto Natales
South to Torres. One border. The massif appears.
Leave by 7am. 259 miles, one border crossing back into Chile via Paso Río Don Guillermo. Drive west on Ruta 9. In the final stretch, the Torres del Paine massif rises on the horizon — three granite towers above the Patagonian plain. Arrive Puerto Natales by late afternoon. Rest. Tomorrow is the park.
Depart 7:00am
Drive ~259 mi
Border Argentina → Chile
Sleep Puerto Natales
12
Wednesday Jan 19 · Torres del Paine — Day One
Into the park. Lago Grey. Let the scale settle.
Drive into Torres del Paine. Day one is not the hike — it's orientation. Lago Grey and the glacier, which calves ice directly into the water in front of you. Mirador Las Torres from below. Let the place arrive before you put your legs into it. January light until 10pm. Sleep inside the park or at Villa Río Serrano — not Puerto Natales. The trailhead is 1.5 hours each way from Natales; adding 3 hours of driving to an 8-hour hike day is avoidable.
Drive ~70 mi into park
Sleep Villa Río Serrano or park lodge — not Puerto Natales
13
Signature
Thursday Jan 20 · Torres del Paine — Base Las Torres
The towers. They fill the sky. The photos are wrong.
Private transfer to the trailhead by 8am. 12 miles roundtrip, ~8 hours, 3,000ft of gain to a glacial lake directly beneath three granite spires. Every photograph flattens them. In person they occupy your entire field of view. Two days ago you stood at the base of Cerro Torre. Today you stand under the Towers. These are the two greatest mountain views in Patagonia and you saw both.
Depart 8:00am
Hike 12 mi · ~8 hrs · 3,000 ft gain
Sleep Villa Río Serrano or park lodge
14
The Crossing
Friday Jan 21 · Torres del Paine → Punta Arenas
Drive to the Strait. Drop the car. Cross to the island.
Drive south from Torres del Paine to Punta Arenas — ~155 miles. Drop the 4WD with LYS at Punta Arenas. Then: cross the Strait of Magellan at Primera Angostura by bus or transfer — 20 minutes, runs all day. Drive onto Tierra del Fuego. Cross into Argentina. Guanacos on every hillside. The island feels like the edge of something. Sleep in Río Grande. Critical: the Chilean Navy shuts the crossing when wind exceeds safe thresholds. Check the TABSA website the night before. If it's closed, sleep in Punta Arenas and cross first thing.
Drive ~155 mi to Punta Arenas
Car drop LYS — Punta Arenas
Ferry Primera Angostura · 20 min · by bus
Contingency Check TABSA night before
Sleep Río Grande

Finish Line
Saturday Jan 22 · Río Grande → Ushuaia
The Fuegian Andes. Then the Beagle Channel appears below.
Travel south through the Fuegian Andes — the southernmost mountain range on Earth — and drop to the Beagle Channel. Arrive Ushuaia. Walk to the waterfront. You started in the Atacama at 14,000 feet. You made it to the bottom of the world. King crab for dinner. The best Malbec available. The conversation isn't about the trip.
Transfer ~130 mi · bus or private transfer
Sleep Ushuaia

Sunday Jan 23 · Ushuaia → Buenos Aires → Atlanta
End of the world. Then home.
Morning flight USH → AEP. Taxi across Buenos Aires to EZE — allow 5–6 hours minimum between landing at AEP and departing EZE. In between: the Atacama, the Carretera Austral, the Marble Caves, Ruta 265, Cerro Torre, the Towers, the Strait of Magellan, the Fuegian Andes, and the Beagle Channel.

What It Actually Feels Like

Five moments you won't forget

01
4am. Fourteen thousand feet. Steam rising before the sun.
You drove to El Tatio in the dark, half-asleep, in freezing thin air. Then the geysers erupt as the Andes ignite behind them. You stand there for a while. You don't need to say anything.
02
Ruta 265. Cliffside. Turquoise water a hundred feet below.
Nobody told you this road existed. 130 miles along the edge of a cliff above Lago General Carrera, the water an impossible color, the Andes directly overhead. You stop three times because you can't help it.
03
Fitz Roy on the horizon. Growing for an hour before you arrive.
Six hours of steppe and wind and nothing, then a granite spire appears and grows until it fills the windshield. The drive earns the arrival. That's the whole point of Ruta 40.
04
The towers fill the sky. Every photograph has lied to you.
Torres del Paine. Three granite spires above a glacial lake. Eight hours of hiking to stand there. January light until 10pm. Worth every step. Two days ago it was Cerro Torre. These are the two greatest mountain views in Patagonia and you saw both.
05
Ushuaia. Last dinner. The conversation isn't about the trip.
King crab and Malbec at the bottom of the world. It's about everything else. That's what the distance is for.

Eyes Open

The reality check

The hardship is the point. But you should know exactly what you're signing up for.

🚗
Two long drive days
Day 9 (Los Antiguos → El Chaltén, 370 miles) and Day 11 (El Chaltén → Puerto Natales, 259 miles) are real. Leave early, drive steady, arrive before dark. These days ask something of you.
🌬
Patagonian weather doesn't care
January is the best time to go. It is still cold, wet, and windy enough to knock you sideways in Torres del Paine. Pack for the weather. Adjust for the weather. That's part of the deal.
💸
This is not cheap
Budget $4,000–5,400 per person all-in. Flights alone are $1,400–1,800. The 4WD with premium insurance and Punta Arenas drop runs ~$660/person split four ways. Commit to the number before you commit to the trip.
📵
You will be unreachable
Cell service disappears on the Carretera Austral for stretches. A Garmin inReach handles emergencies. For everything else — that's the point.
⚡
The roads are serious
The Carretera Austral is narrow gravel with no guardrails. Patagonian crosswinds on Ruta 40 have rolled high-clearance pickups. Drive deliberately, keep speed down on ripio, and both hands on the wheel when the wind picks up. This is not a highway cruise.
🏔
Altitude is real
San Pedro sits at 7,900ft. El Tatio on Day 3 is 14,000ft — 60% of sea-level oxygen. Going from Atlanta to 14,000ft in 60 hours is textbook Acute Mountain Sickness territory. Get Diamox (Acetazolamide) prescriptions before you leave the US. Start taking it 24 hours before landing in Calama. Hydrate aggressively on Days 1 and 2.
✅
You come back different
Every hard thing about this trip is also the reason it works. The drives, the cold, the remoteness, the early starts — they strip everything else away. What's left is four guys, a 4WD, and the bottom of the world. That's plenty.
